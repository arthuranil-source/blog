# How to Right-Size Your Print Fleet After a Company Expansion

When a company grows, whether through hiring, merging with another business, or opening new offices, the print environment rarely grows in an organized way. Printers get added wherever they fit. Old devices from the acquired company stay plugged in. Nobody knows exactly how many printers exist, who uses them, or what they cost.

The result is a bloated, expensive fleet that costs far more than it should. According to [Datapac's guide on right-sizing printer fleets](https://www.datapac.com/right-sizing-your-printer-fleet/), most organizations significantly overspend on printing simply because they've never taken a structured look at what they actually have and what they actually need.

This guide walks you through every step of the process in plain language. By the end, you'll know exactly how to assess your current fleet, decide what stays and what goes, and build a leaner, smarter print environment that fits your company's new size.

---

> **Key Takeaways**
> - Most companies spend 1-3% of annual revenue on printing, much of it wasted on underused devices (IDC, 2024).
> - A structured print audit is the foundation of any right-sizing effort.
> - Consolidating devices to a ratio of one printer per 8-10 employees typically cuts fleet costs by 20-30%.
> - Hybrid workforces and remote teams require cloud-based print management to avoid orphaned print jobs and security gaps.
> - Managed Print Services (MPS) can reduce total print spend by up to 30% while handling maintenance and supplies automatically.

## Why Does Company Expansion Create a Print Problem?

In 2024, IDC research found that organizations typically spend between [1% and 3% of annual revenue on document output](https://my.idc.com/getdoc.jsp?containerId=US51982024), yet most have no accurate count of how many printers they own. That gap - between what printing costs and how little attention it gets - is exactly where expansion makes things worse.

When a company grows, printing infrastructure expands reactively, not strategically. A new department needs a printer, so one gets ordered. An acquired company comes with 40 devices of varying ages and brands. A new office opens and three printers are installed without anyone checking whether two would be enough. Over time, the fleet becomes a patchwork of overlapping, underused, and mismatched devices.

[KraftBusiness's guide to managing your printer fleet](https://kraftbusiness.com/blog/managing-your-printer-fleet/) describes this pattern well: without a clear ownership structure, every team acquires devices independently, and costs multiply invisibly across the organization.

The problem isn't just expense. An oversized fleet creates:

- **Higher maintenance costs** - More devices mean more service calls, more toner orders, and more IT time spent troubleshooting.
- **Security vulnerabilities** - Unmanaged printers are a documented entry point for data breaches. Devices that nobody tracks are also devices that nobody patches.
- **Compliance risk** - In regulated industries like healthcare, finance, and legal services, unmonitored print activity can create audit failures.
- **Environmental waste** - Unused or poorly placed printers consume electricity around the clock and generate unnecessary consumable waste.

Right-sizing isn't about cutting printers for the sake of cutting them. It's about making sure every device in your fleet earns its place.

## Step 1: Conduct a Full Print Audit Before You Make Any Decisions

The single most important thing you can do before moving, removing, or purchasing a single printer is to understand what you currently have. This is called a print audit, and it's the foundation of every successful right-sizing effort.

[Pharos Systems' guide to conducting print audits](https://www.pharos.com/blog/conducting-print-audits/) defines a print audit as a systematic inventory of every output device in your organization, paired with usage data that tells you how much each device is actually being used.

A complete print audit captures:

### What to Document for Every Device

- **Make, model, and age** - Older devices cost more to maintain and are often incompatible with modern print management software.
- **Location** - Which floor, department, or office is the device in? Is it centrally placed or tucked in a corner where few people walk past?
- **Print volume** - How many pages does this device produce per month? Most modern printers log this automatically.
- **Color vs. black-and-white ratio** - Color printing costs 5-10 times more per page than black-and-white. If a device is printing mostly black-and-white, a cheaper mono device might serve that location better.
- **Functionality used** - Is this device being used as a printer only, or do employees also scan and copy on it? A full multifunction device is wasted if it's only ever printing.
- **Who has access** - Is this a shared departmental device or a personal desktop printer? Personal printers are almost always a cost inefficiency.

[Applied Innovation's tips for getting the most from your existing print fleet](https://www.appliedinnovation.com/imaging-services/4-tips-for-getting-the-most-out-of-your-existing-print-fleet/) recommend pulling at least 90 days of usage data before drawing any conclusions. A device that looks idle might have a seasonal spike, and removing it could create problems three months later.

### How to Run the Audit Without Disrupting Work

Most enterprise printers have built-in usage counters accessible through a web browser on the same network. Your IT team can pull these without touching the devices physically. For larger fleets, print management software like PaperCut or Pharos automates the data collection across all networked devices simultaneously.

If your company recently acquired another business, [PaperCut's guide to managing legacy print infrastructure after acquisition](https://www.papercut.com/blog/print_tips/managing-legacy-print-infrastructure-when-acquiring-a-new-business/) recommends treating the acquired fleet as a separate zone during the audit phase. Don't try to integrate the inventories until you have a clear picture of each independently. Legacy devices often run different drivers, different toner types, and different firmware versions, all of which need to be accounted for before consolidation.

> **A practical starting point:** Ask your IT help desk for the last six months of printer-related support tickets. The devices generating the most tickets are the ones costing you the most in hidden labor, not just supplies.

## Step 2: Map Usage Against Location and Headcount

Once you have the audit data, the next step is to compare what you have against what your workforce actually needs. This is where right-sizing decisions get made.

[Kelley Create's guide to sizing your office print fleet](https://kelleycreate.com/size-office-print-fleet/) offers a useful industry benchmark: **one multifunction printer for every 8 to 10 employees** is the standard ratio for most office environments. Desktop personal printers should be the exception, reserved for roles with genuine specialized needs such as legal document production or medical records printing.

### How to Map Your Fleet Against Real Needs

Take your audit data and lay it against your current headcount and floor plan. For each location, answer these questions:

1. **How many employees work in or near this device?** If a printer serves 2 people, it's almost certainly underused. If it serves 25, it may be overwhelmed.

2. **What is the walk distance to the nearest alternative device?** The general guidance from [Marco's guide to consolidating your printer fleet](https://www.marconet.com/blog/bid/334108/work-smarter-consolidate-your-printer-scanner-copier-fleet) is that employees shouldn't have to walk more than 50 feet to reach a shared printer. If two devices are within 20 feet of each other and serve the same team, one of them can almost certainly be removed.

3. **Does this location have any specialized print needs?** A legal department may need a high-volume device with booklet-finishing capability. A reception desk may only need a small color printer for occasional document printing. Match the device specification to the actual requirement, not to what happened to be available when the office was set up.

4. **What happens to this location in the next 12 months?** If a department is growing, removing a device now and adding one back in six months costs more than keeping a slightly underused device in place temporarily. Build expansion plans into your right-sizing model.

### Don't Forget Hybrid and Remote Workers

If your expansion included hiring remote or hybrid employees, your print fleet planning needs to account for a completely different usage pattern. [PaperCut's guide to making printing work in a hybrid environment](https://www.papercut.com/blog/print_tips/how-to-make-printing-work-in-a-hybrid-environment/) notes that hybrid offices often have fluctuating occupancy, meaning a floor that holds 50 desks might have only 20-30 people in the office on any given day.

In that scenario, maintaining the same number of printers you'd need for full occupancy is wasteful. Right-sizing for a hybrid office means modeling for your average daily attendance, not your total headcount.

[3Manager's analysis of printer fleet management in the age of remote work](https://www.3manager.com/post/printer-fleet-management-in-the-age-of-remote-work-challenges-and-solutions) recommends cloud-based print management for any organization with more than 20% of its workforce working remotely on a regular basis. Without a cloud-managed system, print jobs sent by remote workers can sit uncollected on office printers, consuming resources and creating security exposure.

## Step 3: Decide What to Keep, Consolidate, or Replace

With your audit data mapped against location needs, you now have enough information to make decisions about individual devices. Every printer in your fleet falls into one of four categories:

### Category 1: Keep As-Is

This device is well-placed, appropriately sized for the team it serves, and within its useful service life (generally under 5-7 years for a commercial multifunction device). Leave it alone.

### Category 2: Relocate

This device is in the wrong place. Either it's underused because it's poorly positioned, or a different location has a coverage gap. Moving it costs less than buying a new device and removes the inefficiency. This is one of the most commonly overlooked options in fleet right-sizing.

### Category 3: Consolidate

Two or more devices are serving overlapping populations. Remove the lower-performing or older device and let the remaining device absorb the volume. [Sosny's three-step guide to cutting time, money, and risk through printer fleet management](https://sosny.com/printer-fleet-management-3-steps-to-cut-time-money-risk/) identifies consolidation as consistently the highest-return action in any fleet right-sizing exercise. Fewer devices mean lower supply costs, lower maintenance costs, and less IT management overhead.

### Category 4: Replace

This device is old, unreliable, incompatible with your current network infrastructure, or incapable of the functions your team needs. Replacement is warranted, but should be done with a clear specification in mind, not with the default choice from the nearest supplier.

[HTC UK's guide to scaling your fleet with strategic planning](https://www.htc-uk.com/uncategorized/from-small-business-to-enterprise-scaling-your-fleet-with-strategic-planning/) recommends standardizing on as few makes and models as possible across your fleet. When every device uses the same toner cartridge type, the same driver software, and the same maintenance protocol, your supply chain simplifies and your IT team's expertise concentrates rather than fragmenting across dozens of different device configurations.

<!-- [PERSONAL EXPERIENCE] -->
> **What often surprises IT managers:** When they remove personal desktop printers and route those users to shared departmental devices, initial pushback is strong. But within 60-90 days, most users adapt, and the cost savings are immediate and permanent. The resistance is almost always to the change itself, not to the outcome.

## Step 4: Address Compliance and Security Before You Redeploy

Before any device is moved to a new location, connected to a new network segment, or reassigned to a different department, security and compliance requirements must be checked. This is especially true for companies in regulated industries.

In 2025, [Pharos Systems' guide on printing and regulatory compliance](https://www.pharos.com/blog/printing-and-regulatory-compliance-what-audit-ready-actually-looks-like/) documented that printed documents remain one of the most common sources of regulatory compliance failures, yet print infrastructure is often the last thing compliance teams audit. A document containing patient health information or financial account data that sits in an output tray uncollected is a compliance violation regardless of how secure the network transmission was.

### What Compliance Requires from Your Print Fleet

**Secure print release** - Employees authenticate at the printer (using a badge, PIN, or mobile app) before their document prints. This ensures documents are never left uncollected in output trays. [HP's fleet optimization guide](https://www.insights.hpondemand.com/files/static/SaaS/HP_JAInsights_Fleet_Optimization_Guide.pdf) identifies secure print release as the single most effective control for preventing document-level data exposure.

**Full audit logging** - Every print job should be logged with the user, the document type (if detectable), the time, and the device. If your fleet management software doesn't provide this automatically, you'll need to configure it before moving any device into a zone where sensitive data is handled.

**Data at rest encryption** - Many printers store recent print jobs on an internal hard drive. If that device is relocated, decommissioned, or sent for maintenance, that drive needs to be cleared according to your data destruction policy. Older devices often lack the controls to do this automatically.

**Network segmentation** - Printers should not be on the same network segment as endpoints that store sensitive data, unless your security architecture specifically accounts for this. When adding devices to new locations, confirm with your network team that the device placement doesn't create unintended access paths.

[Dectek's deep dive into managed print services processes](https://dectek.ie/mastering-your-print-environment-a-deep-dive-into-decteks-managed-print-services-process/) outlines a practical compliance checklist that can be adapted to most regulated environments.

## Step 5: Implement Print Management Software to Maintain Control

Right-sizing your fleet is a project with a start and end date. Keeping it right-sized is an ongoing process, and that process requires visibility. Without print management software, you're back to guessing within 12-18 months.

[PaperCut's guide to optimizing printer fleet management for medium-sized businesses](https://www.papercut.com/blog/print_tips/optimizing-printer-fleet-management-for-medium-sized-businesses/) defines print management software as a layer of visibility and control that sits between your users and your devices. At minimum, a print management system should show you:

- Real-time device status (online, offline, low toner, paper jam)
- Per-user and per-department print volumes
- Cost per page for each device
- Usage trends over time (so you can spot an underused device before it becomes a problem)

More advanced systems add secure print release, print policy enforcement (such as defaulting to duplex black-and-white to reduce color and paper waste), and integration with your identity management system so that departing employees' print access is automatically revoked.

### Cloud Print Management for Distributed Teams

[Managed Technology's analysis of hybrid working and cloud print management](https://www.managedtechnology.co.uk/hybrid-working-and-the-benefits-of-cloud-print-management/) makes a clear case that cloud-based print management is no longer optional for organizations with more than one office location. Traditional print servers tie print management to physical on-premise infrastructure. When an employee submits a print job from the wrong network location (common in hybrid offices), traditional server-based systems fail silently. Cloud print management routes jobs correctly regardless of where the user is working.

[Quocirca's six strategies for print modernization](https://quocirca.com/content/6-strategies-print-modernisation/) places cloud migration of print management infrastructure among the top priorities for any organization undergoing expansion. The cost of running on-premise print servers, including the hardware, licensing, and IT maintenance time, is consistently higher than equivalent cloud-based solutions over a three-year horizon.

## Step 6: Consider Managed Print Services for Ongoing Optimization

If your company doesn't have dedicated resources to manage print infrastructure on an ongoing basis, Managed Print Services (MPS) is worth serious consideration.

MPS is a service model where a third-party provider takes responsibility for your entire print fleet: the devices, the supplies, the maintenance, and the management software. You pay a predictable per-page or monthly fee, and the provider handles the operational complexity.

[HP's print management white paper](http://www.hp.com/canada/portal/e-assets/productcontent/Whitepapers/HP-PrintManagement.pdf) cites organizations that have reduced total print spend by 20-30% within the first 12 months of switching to an MPS model, primarily through supply chain optimization and proactive maintenance scheduling.

[Lexmark's strategy for managed output adoption](https://www3.universitybusiness.com/mailing/partner/govconnection/2014/Lexmark%20-%20A_Strategy_for_Managed_Output_Adoption.pdf) provides a useful framework for evaluating whether MPS makes sense for a given organization. The general guidance: if printing accounts for more than $50,000 per year in combined device, supply, and maintenance costs, and your IT team spends more than two hours per week on print-related support, MPS will almost certainly deliver a positive ROI within 18 months.

### What MPS Includes (and What It Doesn't)

A standard MPS contract typically covers:

- All toner and consumable supplies, automatically replenished before devices run out
- Preventive maintenance on all contracted devices
- Break-fix service with defined response time SLAs
- Print management software licensing and administration
- Quarterly or annual fleet reviews to identify further optimization opportunities

MPS does not typically include capital purchase of new devices, although most providers offer lease-to-own options as part of the contract structure.

[The SlideShare collection of best practices in printer fleet management](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/best-practices-in-printer-fleet-management/34198873) recommends reading MPS contracts carefully for volume commitments. Some contracts penalize you if your actual print volume is significantly lower than the contracted volume, which can offset savings if your right-sizing exercise reduces printing more than projected.

## How Does Right-Sizing Affect Sustainability Goals?

Company expansions often come with increased scrutiny of environmental impact. Print fleet right-sizing directly contributes to measurable sustainability improvements, which matters both for internal reporting and for external ESG commitments.

[Ricoh Canada's quick facts on sustainable printing](https://www.ricoh.ca/en-CA/insights/articles/quick-facts-sustainable-printing) reports that the average office worker prints approximately 10,000 pages per year, but studies consistently find that 17-45% of those pages are never retrieved from the printer output tray. That's waste that costs money and consumes resources with no productive output.

[EBP Inc.'s guide to greener printing through smarter fleet management](https://www.ebp-inc.com/blog/greener-printing-smarter-fleet-management) identifies three levers that right-sized fleets pull simultaneously:

1. **Fewer devices** - Each printer or copier draws 300-500 watts while running and 10-50 watts in standby. Fewer devices means lower energy consumption around the clock, not just when printing.

2. **Policy enforcement** - Print management software can enforce duplex (double-sided) printing and black-and-white defaults across the fleet. [Canon Europe's sustainable print guide](https://www.canon-europe.com/business/insights/articles/sustainable-print-guide/) reports that duplex defaults alone typically reduce paper consumption by 20-30%.

3. **Pull printing** - Requiring employees to authenticate at the device before their job prints eliminates the substantial category of jobs that are submitted and then forgotten. This single policy change routinely reduces print volume by 10-20%.

<!-- [UNIQUE INSIGHT] -->
> **A tracking approach most companies overlook:** Set a paper consumption baseline in the month before your right-sizing implementation, then measure again at 3 months and 12 months. This gives you a concrete sustainability metric you can report internally, and it also validates whether your right-sizing decisions had the intended effect on behavior.

## What Are the Common Mistakes in Post-Expansion Fleet Right-Sizing?

Understanding the most common mistakes helps you avoid repeating them. Here are the ones that derail right-sizing projects most often.

**Acting without data.** The single most common mistake is making device removal decisions based on assumptions rather than usage data. A printer that looks idle might be the only device capable of handling a specific paper size or finishing function that a department uses twice a month. Always audit before you act.

**Under-communicating with affected employees.** Removing a printer from someone's immediate vicinity without explanation creates resistance and workaround behaviors (such as employees buying personal inkjet printers and expensing the supplies). Communicate the reason, the timeline, and the alternative device location before any changes are made.

**Treating the audit as a one-time project.** [Applied Innovation's guide to getting the most from your print fleet](https://www.appliedinnovation.com/imaging-services/4-tips-for-getting-the-most-out-of-your-existing-print-fleet/) makes the point that a print fleet, like any infrastructure, drifts out of calibration over time. Without a scheduled review, the right-sized fleet of today becomes the over-sized fleet of three years from now. Build a quarterly review into your standard IT operations calendar.

**Ignoring the acquired company's institutional knowledge.** When right-sizing a fleet that includes devices from a recently acquired business, involve someone from that organization in the process. They know why certain devices were placed where they were, and that knowledge can prevent you from removing something that turns out to be critical. [PaperCut's guide on managing legacy infrastructure after acquisition](https://www.papercut.com/blog/print_tips/managing-legacy-print-infrastructure-when-acquiring-a-new-business/) dedicates an entire section to this stakeholder engagement step.

**Standardizing too aggressively, too fast.** Replacing all devices with a single model sounds efficient, but doing it all at once creates a large capital outlay and a wave of user disruption. A rolling replacement schedule tied to existing device end-of-life dates achieves the same standardization goal with a fraction of the disruption.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How many printers should a company have after expansion?

The standard benchmark is one multifunction printer for every 8 to 10 employees in a typical office environment, per [Kelley Create's fleet sizing guide](https://kelleycreate.com/size-office-print-fleet/). Departments with high print volume, such as legal, finance, or document-intensive operations, may warrant a lower ratio of one printer per 5 to 6 employees. Remote and hybrid workers generally don't factor into office device counts.

### What does a print audit cost?

A basic self-conducted audit using built-in device counters and a spreadsheet costs only staff time, typically 8-20 hours for a 50-device fleet. Third-party audit services range from $2,000 to $15,000 depending on fleet size and depth of analysis. [Pharos Systems' audit methodology guide](https://www.pharos.com/blog/conducting-print-audits/) is a practical starting point for conducting an internal audit with no external cost.

### How long does fleet right-sizing take?

For a fleet of 50 to 100 devices across two or three locations, the audit and decision-making phase typically takes 4 to 8 weeks. Physical implementation, meaning device moves and removals, usually happens over 2 to 4 additional weeks in phases to minimize disruption. Larger multi-site implementations can take 3 to 6 months from audit to completion.

### Can we right-size the fleet ourselves, or do we need a vendor?

Most organizations can conduct the audit and make the consolidation decisions independently using their own IT team. Where vendors add the most value is in physical logistics (particularly for large device moves or replacements) and in configuring print management software. [Sosny's three-step fleet management guide](https://sosny.com/printer-fleet-management-3-steps-to-cut-time-money-risk/) offers a self-directed methodology that works well for fleets under 100 devices.

### What happens to decommissioned printers?

Printers removed from service should not simply be stored or thrown away. Options include: returning leased devices to the leasing company, donating to schools or nonprofits (if the device is still serviceable), selling through a certified electronics reseller, or recycling through a manufacturer take-back program. Hard drives inside decommissioned devices must be wiped or physically destroyed before the device leaves your control.

## Conclusion

Right-sizing a print fleet after company expansion is a straightforward process when you follow it in the right order: audit first, decide based on data, address compliance before redeployment, implement software to maintain visibility, and review on a regular schedule.

The organizations that do this well don't treat it as a one-time cleanup project. They build print fleet management into their standard operational rhythm, reviewing usage data quarterly and adjusting as the business changes. That ongoing attention is what keeps costs controlled and the fleet genuinely right-sized over time.

If you're starting from zero after a recent expansion, the audit is the place to begin. Everything else follows from having accurate data about what you actually have and what your people actually use.

- Map usage data against headcount and floor plans
- Apply the four-category decision framework (keep, relocate, consolidate, replace)
